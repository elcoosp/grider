Grider reads an image, binarizes it with an adaptive threshold, detects the runs of background and content scan lines on both axes, and returns them as a [`Grid`](/reference/grid/) of [`Row`](/reference/lines-and-cells/)s and `Column`s — every band tagged `Empty` (background) or `Full` (content).

<Code lang="toml" code={`[dependencies]
grider = "0.3"`} />

<Code lang="rust" code={`use grider::{Grid, GridConfig};
use image::open;

let img = open("path/to/image.png").expect("Failed to open image");
let grid = Grid::try_from_image_with_config(&img, GridConfig::default()).expect("Failed to create grid");

for row in grid.rows() {
    println!("Row at y: {} ({:?})", row.y, row.kind);
}`} />

<Aside kind="tip">
        The `drawing` feature renders this visualization of the detected grid with one call to
        [`debug::save_image_with_grid`](/reference/drawing/) — the figure above shows the real output of the
        `grider` command-line tool on a screenshot fixture.
</Aside>

## Why Grider

- **One-line extraction** — [`Grid::try_from_image_with_config`](/reference/grid/) returns every row and
        column band of the image, tagged `Empty` or `Full`.
- **Fast by construction** — binarization runs on a streaming sliding-window integral image
        (1.6–3.1× faster than the previous `imageproc`-based path, bit-for-bit identical output), the
        emptiness scan is one fused cache-friendly pass, and both steps parallelize over row bands with
        work-size gating. See [Performance](/explanation/performance/).
- **Two binarizers** — the adaptive mean threshold (Bradley & Roth) for clean input, or
        [Sauvola](/guides/choose-binarization/) for degraded documents, both `O(width × height)` regardless
        of the window radius.
- **Ergonomic API** — builders for `GridConfig` and `GridDrawingConfig`, iterators over cells,
        kind-based filters, in-place `retain`, serde support, and visual debug overlays.
